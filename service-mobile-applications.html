When a mobile app is worth building

A mobile app earns its place when people use a service repeatedly, when they need it away from a desk, or when the phone itself makes the job easier — its camera, its location, its ability to notify someone. If none of those apply, a well-built responsive website often serves better and costs less to run.

We will say which of those we think applies to your idea before any development starts.

Put your services in your customers' hands

What people should actually be able to do once the app is on the phone.

Sign In Once, Stay Signed In

One account across your app and your web system, so a customer's history, addresses and saved details are already there.

Notifications Worth Keeping On

An order is out for delivery, a job is assigned, a booking is tomorrow. Tied to real events, so the app does not get muted.

The Phone's Own Abilities

Camera for proof of delivery, location for the nearest branch, saved documents for offline reference — used where the task needs them, not for show.

Usable on a Weak Signal

Where staff work in basements, warehouses or on the road, entries are captured on the device and synced when the connection returns.

Tools for Staff, Not Just Customers

The same app family can carry a private side for your team — today's jobs, status updates, sign-offs at the door.

Comfortable One-Handed

Reachable controls and readable text on a small screen, checked on real handsets rather than a desktop preview.

Support your app beyond the screen

Most of a mobile product is not the app. These are the parts that sit behind it.

Web Applications

The back office you use to run the app — view orders, assign jobs, update the catalogue, answer a customer. Usually built alongside the app, sharing one database.

Custom Software

The backend that holds accounts, business rules and data. Where the same logic has to serve an app, a website and an internal system, it is designed once.

Software Maintenance

Android and iOS release new versions every year and can drop older ones. An unmaintained app eventually stops being accepted by the stores.
