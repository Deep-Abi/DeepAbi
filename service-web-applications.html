Web Applications

Web Applications That Run Your Daily Operations

Software your team opens in a browser to do the work of the business — record, check, approve, report. Nothing to install, and it works from an office computer, a laptop at home or a phone on site.

Discuss Your Web Application See What We Build

Not a website, and not a subscription product

An informational website tells people about your business. A web application lets them do something: place an order, raise a request, approve a leave form, check where a job has got to. The difference matters because it changes what has to be built — accounts, permissions, stored data, and rules about who may see and change what.

It is also different from a SaaS product. A web application is built for one organisation and its way of working. A SaaS product serves many separate customers on one platform. If that is what you have in mind, the SaaS Products page covers it.

Bring your daily operations into one system

The practical difference a web application makes to a working day.

One Record, Not Five Copies

An order lives in one place. Sales, accounts and dispatch read the same record, so nobody reconciles a spreadsheet against an email thread.

People See What They Should

A supervisor approves, a technician updates, a client sees only their own job. Permissions are set per role rather than trusted to convention.

The Current Position, on Screen

Open jobs, pending approvals, this week's numbers — the question “where is that?” gets answered by looking, not by asking.

Steps That Hand Themselves On

When one stage finishes the next person is notified and the record moves. The workflow is in the system, not in somebody's memory.

Custom Software

When the requirement reaches past the browser — a desktop tool at a counter, or a scheduled job moving data overnight — it is scoped as one custom system rather than two disconnected builds.

AI & Automation
